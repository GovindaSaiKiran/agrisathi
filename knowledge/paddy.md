# Paddy Knowledge Base
Paddy cultivation is a highly structured process divided into distinct growth phases. To maximize yield and minimize losses, precise management of water, nutrients, pests, and diseases is required at each stage. Here is a comprehensive, end-to-end guide to paddy cultivation, detailing the exact practices, chemicals, and fertilizers to use from seed to harvest.

## Phase 1: Pre-Planting & Nursery Stage (Days 0 to 25)
The foundation of a healthy crop begins with seed selection and a robust nursery bed.

### 1. Seed Selection & Treatment
- **Salt Water Test**: Dissolve 60 grams of salt in 1 liter of water and soak your seeds. Discard any seeds that float, as they are hollow or diseased. Dry the sunken, healthy seeds in the shade.
- **Chemical Treatment (Fungicide)**: Treat seeds with Carbendazim 50% WP or Captan/Thiram at 2 to 4 gm per kg of seed for 24 hours. This protects against seed-borne diseases like early blast.
- **Organic/Bio-Control (Optional)**: Treat seeds with Pseudomonas fluorescens at 10 gm per kg of seed. Note: Do not mix bio-agents with chemical fungicides.

### 2. Nursery Preparation
- **Fertilizer**: For a 1000m² nursery area (sufficient for 1 hectare of main field), apply 10 kg Nitrogen (N), 2 kg Phosphorus (P2O5), and 2 kg Potassium (K2O).
- **Sowing**: Sow the pre-germinated seeds. Keep the soil saturated but do not submerge completely until seedlings establish.
- **Care**: If seedlings turn yellow, it may indicate zinc deficiency; spray a 0.5% Zinc Sulphate solution.

## Phase 2: Vegetative Phase (Transplanting to Maximum Tillering)
This phase focuses on root establishment and encouraging the plant to produce as many tillers (branches) as possible.

### 1. Land Preparation & Basal Fertilizer (Main Field)
- **Ploughing**: Puddle the soil thoroughly.
- **Basal Fertilizer Dose (Per Hectare)**: Apply 10 tonnes of Farm Yard Manure (FYM). For the wet season, apply 80:40:40 kg of N:P:K (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium) per hectare. In zinc-deficient soils, apply 25 kg/ha of Zinc Sulphate as a basal application.

### 2. Transplanting
- Transplant seedlings when they are 20 to 25 days old (or 10-14 days for SRI method).
- Maintain a spacing of 20 x 15 cm for standard planting.

### 3. Weed Management
- **Pre-emergent (3 days after transplanting)**: Apply Pretilachlor 50% EC (500-800 ml/acre) to stop weed seeds from germinating.
- **Post-emergent (15-20 days after transplanting)**: If weeds appear, apply Bispyribac Sodium 10% SC (80-100 ml/acre).

### 4. Water Management
- Maintain a shallow water depth of 2 to 5 cm. Deep water reduces the number of tillers the plant can produce.
- Drain the water completely for about a week after the maximum tillering stage to encourage stronger root growth, then reflood.

### 5. Mid-Tillering Fertilizer
- Apply the remaining split of Nitrogen (Urea) around 30 to 40 days after transplanting to boost tiller growth.

## Phase 3: Reproductive Phase (Panicle Initiation to Flowering)
This is the most critical stage. Any stress (water, nutrient, pest) here directly reduces your grain yield.

### 1. Water & Nutrient Management
- Ensure continuous water presence (do not drain the field). Water stress during flowering leads to empty grains (chaff).
- Apply the final split of Nitrogen at the panicle initiation stage (when the stem starts to swell, known as the "booting" stage).

## Phase 4: Ripening & Harvesting
### 1. Water Management
- Stop irrigation and drain the field completely 10 to 15 days before the expected harvest date. This accelerates grain ripening and hardens the soil for harvesting machinery.

## Complete Pest & Disease Management Guide
Below is a breakdown of the major threats, how to identify them, and the exact chemical cures.

### Major Pests & Chemical Cures

| Pest Name | Identification / Symptoms | Chemical Cure & Dosage (Per Acre) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Yellow Stem Borer** | Central shoot dries up ("dead heart"). Later, panicles emerge white and empty. | Broadcast Chlorantraniliprole 0.4% GR (Ferterra) at 4 kg/acre. |
| **Gall Midge** | Base of the tiller swells into a hollow, silvery tube (onion leaf symptom); plant stops growing. | Spray Quinalphos 25% EC at 2 ml per liter of water. |
| **Brown Plant Hopper (BPH)** | Insects cluster at the base of the plant. Crop dries up in circular patches (hopper burn). | Spray Pymetrozine 50% WG at 120-150 gm/acre, directing the spray at the base of the plant. |
| **Leaf Folder** | Caterpillars fold leaves longitudinally and scrape the green tissue inside, leaving white streaks. | Spray Profenofos 50% EC at 100 ml/acre or Flubendiamide. |
| **Rice Hispa** | Small, spiny beetles scrape the upper surface of leaves, causing papery white streaks. | Spray Chlorpyriphos 50% + Cypermethrin 5% EC at 1.5 ml per liter of water. |

### Major Diseases & Chemical Cures
*Warning: Avoid over-applying Nitrogen (Urea), as lush green canopies attract severe fungal infections.*

| Disease Name | Identification / Symptoms | Chemical Fungicide & Dosage (Per Acre) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rice Blast / Neck Blast** | Spindle-shaped brown spots with grey centers on leaves. Later, the neck of the panicle rots and breaks. | Spray Azoxystrobin + Tebuconazole (Custodia) at 300 ml/acre or Tricyclazole. |
| **Sheath Blight** | Greyish-green lesions on the leaf sheath near the water line, later turning blackish-brown. | Spray Propiconazole 25% EC at 200 ml/acre. |
| **Brown Spot** | Numerous small, oval, dark brown spots on the leaves; can cause the whole leaf to dry up. | Spray Hexaconazole 5% EC at 200 ml/acre. |
| **False Smut** | Individual grains on the panicle transform into large, velvety greenish-black spore balls. | Spray Azoxystrobin + Difenoconazole (Amistar Top) at 200 ml/acre during the booting stage. |
| **Bacterial Leaf Blight** | Leaves turn yellow/straw-colored starting from the tip and edges with wavy margins. | Spray Streptomycin Sulphate + Tetracycline (Plantomycin/K-mycin) at 6 to 10 gm per 50 liters of water. |
